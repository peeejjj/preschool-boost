Attachment, Trauma and Child Development, in full

A professional development series for early childhood educators, built on attachment theory, developmental neuroscience and trauma-informed practice. Teams learn to read attachment patterns and stress responses in children aged three to five, create environments that communicate safety, use co-regulation and emotional literacy in the moment, and embed therapeutic approaches into everyday routines. It is grounded in current research and clinical experience and designed for the whole team, so that the change lasts.

  1. Foundations of attachment and development
  2. Understanding trauma in the early years
  3. Creating safe and predictable environments
  4. Co-regulation and emotional literacy
  5. Embedding therapeutic practice into daily routines
  6. Engaging families through an attachment lens

How does a term run?

Six units delivered fortnightly across one school term, starting in Week 1. On the alternate weeks, an optional 90-minute group supervision session helps your team apply each unit in your rooms. Units build on each other, so we recommend the full series, but you can book any number in any order.

Can it be adapted for families?

Yes. The whole series can be delivered to families, or to a mixed group of educators and families. Ask on the planning call.

Learn to Play, in full

Play is not a luxury. It is the work of childhood. Learn to Play is a structured, therapist-led group program based on Dr Karen Stagnitti’s internationally recognised approach, brought into your preschool room. It is for children who are not yet playing to their developmental potential, and it builds the blocks of pretend play: object substitution, doll and teddy play, role play, and the ability to create and sustain a play story. Those skills underpin language, social development and school readiness.

Which children benefit most?

Children who seem stuck in their play, who repeat the same simple themes, who find it hard to join other children’s play stories, or whose communication is still emerging. That includes children with developmental delays, autism, language difficulties or trauma histories. Children are chosen for where they are in their play, not for their behaviour.

How is the group chosen?

Your educators nominate the children they think would benefit. Our therapist confirms the group at the planning visit, so that the children are at a similar stage of pretend play and the group works for everyone in it.

What do educators take away?

One clear play strategy each week, modelled in the group and discussed afterwards, plus language modelling techniques and growing confidence to run imaginative play themselves. By the end, your educators can run the group without us.

Evidence: Learn to Play maps to all five AEDC domains. Its primary domain on the Preschool Boost Menu is communication skills and general knowledge. See AkindaCo on the Preschool Boost Menu.

Allied health supports, in full

Our therapists work in your centre with groups of children and the educators around them: observing rooms and routines, running small groups, modelling strategies and coaching your team. Preschool Boost funds group and capacity-building work, not individual assessment or therapy, and we hold to that.

What could it look like?

A block of visits around a goal you set, for example play environments, transitions, regulation in the room, or supporting a group of children with emerging communication. We scope it with you on the planning call and quote it by the hour, so it fits the allocation you have.

Expectations

What we’ll ask of your centre

Not much, but the few things we ask make the difference between a program that sticks and one that drifts.

For the training

  • A room that fits your team, with a projector or a TV we can plug a laptop into. If there is neither, we bring printed slides.
  • Educators released for the three hours of each unit, and 90 minutes for each supervision session if you add them.
  • One contact person for dates, room set-up and invoicing.

For Learn to Play

  • A play space for 8 to 10 children, and a quiet spot for the educator sessions before and after.
  • The same two educators every week, in the room for the full two hours. They are co-facilitators, so the session cannot cover breaks or ratios.
  • Help choosing the children, and the same day and time each week.
  • A few everyday toys set out from the short list we send the day before. We bring the Learn to Play kit and the weekly handout.

For everything

  • Someone who can confirm the quote in writing.
  • A finance contact who can set AkindaCo up as a supplier. Doing this at booking saves a term of chasing later.
  • As much notice as you can give if a session cannot go ahead.

Logistics

Setting up, term by term

When can we start?

Training runs by school term from Week 1, in the Tuesday evening or Wednesday morning stream. Learn to Play runs weekly in a morning slot (9:30 to 11:30 am) or an afternoon slot (1:30 to 3:30 pm). Wednesday mornings fill first. 2027 terms are booking now.

How long does set-up take?

We confirm your trainer or therapist six weeks before you start, check your room and equipment four weeks out, and your invoice is due two weeks out. Booking a term ahead keeps everything comfortable.

Holidays and public holidays?

Learn to Play pauses over the term break at no charge and picks up in Week 1 of the next term. Each block has a make-up week in hand for a public holiday or closure, so it still finishes inside the term.

Two years, or two rooms?

The two Learn to Play blocks are always consecutive terms, so Terms 3 and 4 this year or Terms 1 and 2 next year both work. Within a week the group can rotate between rooms or age groups.

What if a session is cancelled?

If we cancel, for example because your therapist is unwell, we make it up at the end of the block at no charge. If your centre cancels with less than two business days’ notice, the session is charged. If a program cannot be finished, the remaining paid sessions become educator training, coaching, or a play-environment consultation.

What do we get at the end?

An end-of-program summary for your Preschool Boost reporting, CPD certificates for every educator who took part, certificates for the children in the Learn to Play group, and a team who can keep the play group going.

Good questions

The things directors ask us first

Is the 17 weeks for both programs?

No. Each program stands alone. The training is six 3-hour units across one term. Learn to Play is 17 weekly program visits plus a set-up visit, across two consecutive terms.

Is the price per educator?

No. Every price is per session for your whole team. A training unit is the same price whether four educators attend or twenty-four.

Can we book just one training unit?

Yes. Any number of units, in any order. We recommend the full series because each unit builds on the last, and the full-series price saves you the cost of one unit.

How many staff can attend the training?

As many as you like. For teams larger than 25 we add a second facilitator at no extra charge so that everyone gets time in the activities.

Can training run after hours, or in a staff meeting?

Yes. The Tuesday evening stream runs 5:30 to 8:30 pm, which suits most staff-meeting nights. The Wednesday morning stream suits non-contact time. Supervision sessions can run online at a time you choose, including early evening.

Can any of it be online?

Training units and supervision can run online or in your centre. Learn to Play is always in your room, because your educators are in the group with the children.

We are outside metro Adelaide. Can you still come?

Yes. Training can run online, or as a blended model with the first unit online, units two to five in a two-day visit, and the last unit and supervision online. Travel beyond 10 km of North Adelaide or Salisbury is charged by time at the hourly rate, and for regional centres we quote flights and accommodation separately. Learn to Play in regional centres is by arrangement.

Can Learn to Play run fortnightly, or for fewer weeks?

No. Learn to Play is 17 program weeks plus a set-up visit, weekly, across two consecutive terms, because 17 weekly sessions is what the research behind the program is built on. Fortnightly and shorter runs lose momentum for the children. If your allocation only reaches half the program this year, book a starter block, which is the set-up visit and the first eight program weeks, and complete the program in your next Preschool Boost plan.

Can we hold dates before our Preschool Boost plan is approved?

Yes. We pencil dates in with your provisional booking and confirm once your plan is approved. Nothing is invoiced until you confirm in writing.

Our group is bigger than ten children. Does that matter?

The program works best with 8 to 10 children at a similar stage of play. With two educators in the room we can work with up to about 14. A whole room of 20 or more needs to be split, and we can help you choose.

Can staff who miss a training unit catch up?

Yes. We send the slides ahead of time so they can review them before the next supervision session, and with the consent of everyone in the room a unit can be recorded for staff who could not attend.

What do we get for our Preschool Boost reporting?

An end-of-program summary, attendance records, CPD certificates for educators and certificates for the children in the Learn to Play group.

Does this count as on-menu spending for a Flying Start partner service?

Yes. All three are approved items on the OECD Preschool Boost Menu, listed until June 2028, so they count toward the on-menu share of your allocation. Individual child assessment or therapy is outside the scope of Preschool Boost, and we do not offer it under this funding.

Can we use allied health supports for one child?

No. Preschool Boost funds group and capacity-building work, so our therapists work with groups of children and with your educators. A child who needs individual assessment or therapy is referred through their family and the NDIS or Medicare, outside Preschool Boost.

Who delivers the programs?

AkindaCo’s multidisciplinary team of therapists, counsellors and developmental educators, all with current Working with Children Checks, and covered by public liability and professional indemnity insurance. You will have a named Preschool Boost coordinator at hello@akindaco.com for the whole program.
